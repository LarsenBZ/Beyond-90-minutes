Is It Expected for a Club to Let Their Star Go? The Case of Julian Alvarez

By Braulio Zelaya · August 2026 · 5 min read

A new beginning

The Argentinian player Julian Alvarez would be transferred from Manchester City to Atlético Madrid in August 2024. Julian would sign a contract that expired until June 2030 with an estimated release clause of €500m. Julian saw this as a way to escape from being the shadow of Haaland at Manchester City, but it would end up becoming a nightmare for the player. Julian Alvarez would quickly become a star at Atlético Madrid. Julian has had great performances against Real Madrid (5-2 Metropolitano 25/26), against Barcelona in Champions League and Copa Del Rey (25/26) among other performances. While at Atlético Madrid from August 2024 to current August 2026 Julian Alvarez would win 0 trophies while Barcelona and Real Madrid continued to compete for the top, Atlético remained stuck in third place.

Is this enough for Julian Alvarez?

From his transfer to Manchester City from River Plate to his transfer to Atlético Madrid from Manchester City, Julian Alvarez has shown that he wants to prove that he is one of the best. Julian did not want to be the shadow of Haaland and now in his view he wants to win at a major club while being the star.

Julian Alvarez to Barcelona?

During the World Cup on June 23rd, moments after Argentina's 2-0 win over Austria, Julian Alvarez stated publicly that he wanted to leave the club to "fulfill his dream" and that a transfer was "the best for everyone involved" — a move to Barcelona (Sky Sports; Sports Illustrated). By analysts and journalists this was seen as a strategy to put pressure on the club for his exit. The goal? Join Barcelona to become the starting striker after the exit of Lewandowski.

"Julián has a dream, but we Atlético fans have dreams, too."
— Atlético CEO Miguel Ángel Gil Marín, to EFE

Atlético Madrid's answer: Pay the release clause

Paying the release clause makes it so that the two clubs don't have to negotiate. The club interested in the player just needs to pay the amount and later negotiate with the player. However, this is impossible for not only Barcelona, for any club paying €500m is just not a plan. Club president Enrique Cerezo made the club's position plain: whoever wants Álvarez can "come and look at the contract" — the release clause or nothing (Fox Sports). This would become the saga of Julian Alvarez as Barcelona would try but Atlético Madrid would persist.

Is it fine for a club to keep their star player from a rival transfer?

There are two main sides those who believe that Atlético Madrid is acting as a big club and that it isn't. Arguing that clubs like Atlético Madrid are a step to go to an European giant. Others argue that to become a big club you must act like one and that Atlético Madrid will not be able to surpass Barcelona and Real Madrid if they sell their stars. Similar to how Bayern Munich buy the Bundesliga stars while the other clubs stay well below the capacity to compete against Bayern long term.

What happens now?

Recent reports from journalists such as Fabrizio Romano state an approach from Arsenal, but this seems to not be an option. Julian Alvarez seems to likely be forced to stay at Atlético Madrid while his environment claim that there were broken promises. Julian will have to play and regain the love of the Atlético Madrid fans. Forgiveness will not be enough but just like with the principito Antoine Griezmann when he signed for Barcelona forgiveness is granted by showing passion for badge. The colchoneros wishing for him to leave will celebrate with him once again if Julian is able to show the talent he has. However, the question still persists, is the authority over the future of players such as the case Julian Alvarez morally correct?
