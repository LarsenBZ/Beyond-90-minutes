Has VAR Made Football Worse?

By Braulio Zelaya · August 2026 · 4 min read

There is a football before and after refereeing with the assistance of VAR. VAR is a tool that assists the referee in certain situations, allowing them to review a play if the VAR officials think the referee on the pitch should take another look at their decision.

When is VAR used?

  • Goals and their build-up — checking whether the offensive side committed a foul, an offside, or a handball leading to the goal.
  • Penalty decisions — the referee has to make a decision in the moment. If the VAR officials see something the referee on the pitch didn't, that could change the decision, VAR will call the referee to the review area. The referee then decides whether their original call stands or not.
  • Direct red cards — VAR can call the referee for a review if it spots something that could change the decision.
  • Mistaken identity — cases where the referee penalizes the wrong player. This is looked at by VAR and corrected.

How can a tool built to make football more "fair" end up making it worse?

Most football fans will likely agree that VAR is more "fair," but it can remove the emotion from the game. Before VAR, even if a goal was offside or not, if the linesman didn't raise their flag then fans could celebrate with all the emotion in the world. Now, if there's even a chance of an offside in the play, it's difficult to celebrate. Many times the situation becomes goal → celebration → wait → VAR lines → correct frame of offside → decision → celebrate again. The gap between the goal and the final decision can be fast, but also really slow, especially in smaller leagues. The VAR offside lines specifically can have errors too — even with the semi-automated system, a person still has to choose the correct frame from when the player passes the ball, which can lead to mistakes. VAR disrupts the flow of the game, many times killing the tension or emotion in the moment.

"VAR is killing the raw emotion of the game."
— Andros Townsend, to the BBC

This isn't just a fan complaint — players and managers have said the same thing. Even Pep Guardiola, whose teams have benefited from VAR as often as they've been hurt by it, admitted the celebration itself changes: "You must be mentally strong when VAR is not on our side," he said after a City game decided by review. Also, in other instances such as handballs and penalties, fans still argue about the referee's decision, since it's still within the referee's criteria for what the correct call is. VAR removes some doubt, but not all of it — and it asks fans to hold their emotion hostage until it's sure.

Moments that would have changed football with VAR

With VAR, an amazing or historical goal can be overturned in a way it wouldn't have been without it. The biggest moment that could have changed football history with VAR would have been Maradona's handball against England, which gave Argentina the lead to make the game 1-0. Without that goal — which would almost certainly be overturned by VAR today — history could have looked very different, and Argentina may not have become World Champions for the second time. There are many other cases, big or small: offside goals, mistaken penalties, or even goalkeepers who dived well off their line at penalties and got away with it because it was too difficult to regulate at the time.

Is VAR healthy for football?

Yeah, but not in its current state. VAR still has too many mistakes that need to be polished with newer technology, and that technology needs to be adapted for all major leagues at minimum. A fairer game will always be better, but the time between decisions needs to be reduced, along with the chance of errors. Overall, football officiating will keep changing as society changes through technology. The best thing football can do is adapt for the health of the game. Different doesn't mean worse or better — it's the way it's managed that decides whether the impact is positive or negative.
